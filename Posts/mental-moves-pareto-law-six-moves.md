## Mental Moves and software systems

This post follows up on [Signals & Levers in Practice: A Software-Architecture Case
Study](signals-and-levers). That post introduced several systems-thinking exercises
that I have used over the years. This one will look more closely at the exercises
described by the Cabreras as [Mental Moves](https://www.stsi.pro/mentalmoves),
particularly the six moves included in their **Pareto Law of Six Moves**.

### Framing notes

- Explain why a small set of repeatable mental moves can provide a useful starting point for analyzing complicated systems.
- Introduce the six moves from the Cabreras' framework without treating them as a substitute for understanding the system being examined.
- Apply each move to software architecture, delivery, reliability, or another concrete engineering situation supplied during the full draft.
- Connect the exercises to signals, levers, feedback loops, delays, and other systems-thinking concepts from the previous post.
- Close with practical questions for deciding which move to try when a problem is difficult to reproduce or has no single obvious cause.

The complete article should name and explain each of the six moves from the source,
then show how the exercises change the questions an engineer asks about a system.
It should preserve the distinction between describing the Cabreras' framework and
my own applications of it.
